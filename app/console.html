Approvals

With human-in-the-loop enabled, the system proposes a sortie and a qualified operator authorises it. Routine flights go without asking you, and are logged. The system stops and asks a person only on named grounds — marginal expected benefit, a routing through a threat envelope, spending a launch point down to its last unit of blood or plasma, or serving a lower-acuity casualty while an IMMEDIATE is still unassigned. Escalated proposals expire after 8 minutes of simulated time: hesitation has a cost, and the log records it.

THE PLANNING STANDARD

The Golden Hour was a promise the helicopters kept. In the next fight they cannot keep it.

Sixty minutes to surgery is what the force is designed around. Four figures say it will not hold.

1

It cannot be kept in the Pacific.

Air ambulances met it in uncontested skies. The Pacific assumption is days, not hours.

60 minwhat rests on the number

Force design, where the surgeons sit, what a medic carries, how many aircraft a division holds — all of it is built on one number, sixty minutes from wounding to surgical care. That standard was met in Iraq and Afghanistan by air ambulances flying in uncontested skies. It is not going to be met in the Pacific, and the people who plan this already know it.

It was the wrong number to begin with.

A wearable gives each casualty their own deadline, 16 to 25 minutes before collapse.

an average, not a measurementwhere the number came from

Sixty minutes was never a measurement. It was an average, turned into a standard because a standard was needed and nothing better could be measured at the point of wounding. Now something better can be.

Measure a battle's worth of casualties that way and the Golden Hour does not merely fail to be met. It turns out to have been wrong for nearly every individual it was applied to, in both directions at once.

Every casualty's own deadline, against the one clock they all share
3

One clock each — and the medical plan becomes computable.

Deadlines plus known reach makes this a scheduling problem, and it has an answer.

a number, not an estimatewhat a commander could be told

Once every casualty carries a deadline and every aircraft carries a known capability, a commander can be told — before the fight — how many people a given force laydown will lose, which constraint is killing them, and what specifically to change. Not an estimate. A number, from the same model that flies the mission.

The case beyond lives

Fewer dead is the reason to do this. It is not the number that wins the argument, because it is small and contested. These three are large, uncontested, and measured from the same run: the blood you did not destroy, the evacuation demand you did not create, and the flying you did not have to do. Every figure is per 1 000 casualties so it scales to a theatre honestly.

Doctrine retrieval

Ask in plain English. A sentence encoder running on this machine, offline, matches the question against reference passages and quotes the closest sentence back with its similarity score. It has no ability to generate text, which is exactly why it cannot invent a source. The passages are summaries written for this prototype — read the notice below before you read anything else.

the barwhat launches on its own

Clear-cut sorties above the bar launch immediately — a human bottleneck on the obvious costs lives. Marginal ones below it, typically a scarce blood unit spent on a low-yield case or a route through a threat envelope, wait for a person. Raise the bar and more decisions land in the queue.

Comparison
FairRealistic

Fair gives the control perfect triage — a baseline no real unit achieves. Realistic applies START's published 57.8% sensitivity and 26% over-triage.

Same seeded stream and outcome draws in both arms, so the difference is the tasking decision.

Off

When enabled, the uploaded short-flight telemetry contributes only a deterministic distribution of flight-time variation. It does not replace or validate published platform speed, payload, range, weather, or reliability. Off preserves the published reference run exactly; changing this setting restarts both arms.

Enabled

Modelled policy proposal — not a fielded capability.

82%of a Tier 3 combat medic

The aircraft relays augmented-reality guidance so a Combat Lifesaver can administer freeze-dried plasma and TXA under remote medic supervision, at 82% of the efficacy of a Tier 3 combat medic. Switch it off to see the result without it — this is the single largest lever in the model, and it is a training-and-authority decision, not a technology one.

at equal clinical benefitthe weighting rule

Under METT-TC a commander decides, before contact, which capabilities the mission cannot replace inside the fight. ANGEL SWARM serves those casualties ahead of queue order at equal clinical benefit — the weighting is applied after the break-even test, so a designation changes who is served first and never manufactures a sortie that was not worth flying.
